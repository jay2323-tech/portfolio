who: DesiFit — a fitness/product surface where user health-adjacent data, consent, and retention rules have to be designed in, not patched on after launch.
constraints:
  - India’s Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act shapes what can be collected, why, and for how long.
  - "Solo-built product decisions — every new field is a compliance liability."
  - Users must understand consent in plain language — not a 40-page policy dump.
  - Prefer minimizing data over “collect everything for later ML.”
architectureSummary: Product flows collect only what a feature needs; consent and purpose are recorded at capture time; retention and deletion paths are explicit. Architecture favors clear data boundaries between profile, activity, and any third-party integrations so a deletion request is executable, not aspirational.
decisions:
  - title: Purpose limitation over analytics maximalism
    body: Declined “just in case” event tracking that would widen the personal-data surface. Less fuel for growth dashboards; fewer nightmares under a DPDP inquiry.
  - title: Consent as product UI, not a footer link
    body: Built consent and purpose copy into the flow where data is requested. Legal text still exists — but the product has to explain itself at the moment of collection.
  - title: Deletion as a real path
    body: Designed account/data deletion as a feature with a clear backend path early. Retrofitting erasure onto a tangled data model is how compliance projects explode.
whatBroke: Early wireframes assumed a typical SaaS analytics stack. Mapping those events against “purpose” and retention made half the tracking plan indefensible. The reset hurt velocity for a week and saved a rewrite later. Compliance is an architecture constraint, not a checklist at the end.

DesiFit is where product ambition meets DPDP reality — consent-first data design for a fitness experience that has to be able to explain, limit, and delete what it holds.
